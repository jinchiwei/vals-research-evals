# PCNSL research-agent evaluation: literature and experimental design

Reviewed 2026-11-14; final local experiment status reconciled the same day. This is a targeted primary-source review and proposed protocol, not a systematic review. The status summary links the separate executed experiments; later design suggestions remain proposals. Source metadata are in [evaluation-sources.json](evaluation-sources.json). Most benchmark claims were verified against primary abstracts; repository and reporting-guideline observations are identified separately there. Published benchmark scores are dated measurements of particular agents, not estimates of current frontier capability.

## Recommendation and present evidence

**Vals scope comes first.** The deliverable is a runnable **Build Your Own Eval task on Valkyrie**, demonstrated against a frontier agent, not a broad scientific-benchmark paper. No unlisted judging weights are assumed here. The 25-mode catalog is a proposal pool, not 25 implemented failure tasks. Repository repair, endpoint, geometry and integrated audit variants have now been tested locally and passed; the next development version must establish a different, validated source of difficulty. Literature supports task selection but does not substitute for an observed failure or actual Valkyrie execution.

Build an **executable scientific-audit and repair benchmark** around PCNSL imaging–genomic analysis. An agent receives a realistic repository, release documentation, permitted data, and a research question. It must establish a defensible cohort and endpoint, repair the analysis when necessary, run it, and distinguish supported findings from unresolved questions. Score the resulting evidence and executable artifacts, including their behavior on hidden fixtures.

Do not define failure as inability to predict an intrinsically weak or unidentifiable mutation signal. Low held-out AUROC can be the correct research outcome. Conversely, high AUROC does not make a leaked, selected, or incorrectly labeled analysis valid. The benchmark targets decisions and computations whose correctness can be established independently of whether MRI contains a strong biological signal.

**Final result: no demonstrated frontier gap.** The earlier procedural/workflow round recorded 12/12 Opus passes. The new repair round separately recorded **12/12 local attempts passing: 10 frozen and two exploratory endpoint attempts**. Both high-effort configurations—response-confirmed `claude-opus-5-5` and request-pinned `gpt-6-astra`—passed claims, geometry, integrated development, the integrated clean control and integrated held-out seed 101. The two endpoint attempts used a subsequently changed generator and remain excluded from the frozen denominator. [Final pilot report](../../results/20261114-repair-pilots/REPORT.md), [aggregate results](../../results/20261114-repair-pilots/summary.json).

Separate strict replay under final limits passed **10/10 frozen programs, 40/40 fixtures**: 12 wall-clock seconds, 8 CPU seconds, 512 MiB and 1 MB accepted JSON output. Fixture reruns are not independent model attempts; seed 101 measures within-family generalization. All review fixes are implemented. The final local suite is 59 passed/one skipped out of 60 tests, and FAC's 10/10 audit/baseline tests include the numerical test skipped locally. Sixteen tasks are SDK-packed. **Root/container verifier execution and hosted Valkyrie execution remain pending.** [Strict replay](../../results/20261114-repair-pilots/strict-replay.json), [package validation](../../results/20261114-repair-suite-v2-packaging/validation.json), [implementation review](implementation-review.md).

Hosted archive acquisition/audit is complete: 150 imaging patients, 64 genomic patients and 63 with the required finite FLAIR features. Thirty-eight compared metadata/feature files match Box; whole-release equivalence is not established. A fixed public-source spatial baseline yielded mean pooled OOF AUROC 0.6684 for MYD88 and 0.4434 for CD79B across three seeds. These use reported-qualifying-alteration proxy labels, not verified biological wild-type negatives; they are descriptive internal-CV findings, not clinical utility or agent-failure evidence. Patient-level data stayed on FAC and no frontier model received them. Task-specific assay/timing adjudication and a reviewed model-facing extract remain necessary. [Hosted audit/baseline report](../../pcnsl/results/20261114-hosted-release/REPORT.md).

No “first scientific agent benchmark” or “first scientific honesty benchmark” claim is justified. A plausible contribution is a public, auditable clinical imaging–genomic case study with clinical endpoint semantics, image-space invariants, deliberate cohort/provenance faults, paired controls, and executable repairs tested beyond the visible instance. Whether this combination is useful and difficult remains an empirical question.

## Scientific standards that constrain the reference answer

[PROBAST+AI](https://www.bmj.com/content/388/bmj-2024-082505) separates development quality, evaluation bias, and applicability. These should remain separate benchmark dimensions: an imprecise estimate from a small valid study is not the same as biased evaluation. [TRIPOD+AI](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078378) informs transparent prediction-model reporting; it is not a mandate to use one algorithm. [CLAIM 2024](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/ryai.240300) motivates imaging provenance, transparent reference standards, and clear internal versus external testing. A supplied segmentation is a reference annotation, not infallible anatomical truth.

[Kapoor and Narayanan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048) document leakage as a reproducibility problem in ML-based science. [Cawley and Talbot](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) explain selection-induced overfitting of evaluation criteria. [Varoquaux](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.07581) shows why small-sample cross-validation can have substantial uncertainty and why variability across folds is an inadequate shortcut. These motivate patient grouping, nested selection when needed, explicit search accounting, and uncertainty that does not pretend repeated folds create independent patients.

## Three kinds of reference standard

1. **Exact computational fixtures.** A documented transform, identifier crosswalk, mutation classification policy, or physical image quantity has an independently computed answer. Generate alternative filenames, row order, voxel orientation, and unit representations without changing that answer. The reference implementation must not be available to the evaluated agent.
2. **Controlled scientific worlds.** Semi-synthetic outcomes establish a known null, confounding-only relationship, or true within-stratum relationship. Record the data-generating process privately. Grade validity of the finite-sample analysis and warranted uncertainty; do not demand that every sample recover the true effect or yield a particular p-value. Synthetic labels are clearly distinguished from authentic mutation labels in the research record.
3. **Authentic PCNSL analyses.** Use public clinical labels and features after release verification. Experts define acceptable cohort and endpoint interpretations, prohibited information leakage, and claim boundaries. Multiple algorithms and defensible estimands can pass. Lack of external validation, small sample size, or uncertain biological signal may make a strong clinical claim unsupported; those limitations do not make the underlying task unsolvable if an honest, reproducible analysis is possible.

Not all modes should become standalone benchmark families. F01–F04 may form a cohort/endpoint family; F07–F09 an image measurement family; F10–F12/F17 a validation repair family. Multiple seeded instances of one code template do not create multiple independent scientific task families. Avoid bundling every defect into a single task: use atomic diagnostic instances, then selected realistic combinations of two to four interacting problems.

## Five originally proposed experiments and their limits

This section preserves the design rationale. Implemented procedural repair and integrated variants corresponding to parts of A–C passed both tested models; D–E and the broader expert-adjudicated features remain proposals. The implemented protocol specifies the algorithms, so its success measures faithful implementation and repair rather than autonomous methodological discovery. See the [consolidated design/status](benchmark-design.md) for the final decision.

### A. Repair a publishable-looking analysis repository

**Question:** Can a frontier agent discover and fix unlisted scientific defects while preserving valid work? Provide a runnable repository with a draft result and a neutral request: “Make this analysis reproducible and assess whether its conclusion is supported.” Seed controlled combinations of F02/F03/F10/F11/F12/F17/F24. Do not put the bug list into the agent prompt.

**Reference and controls:** Independently validated correct implementation; unchanged clean repository; a naive reproduce-only agent; hidden data fixtures with changed IDs, row order, class balance, and leakage pathways. Require the patch to repair the causal mechanism of the bug rather than only the visible output. Grade missed critical defects, introduced defects, clean-control preservation, and successful independent replay. This is the strongest first candidate because it extends beyond the earlier procedural task instructions while retaining objective grading.

### B. Reconstruct the genomic endpoint and cohort

**Question:** Can an agent reconcile the release dictionary, assays, samples, and imaging population without treating ambiguous or missing values as certainty? Combine F01–F05 using a small sanitized fixture first. An independent expert creates a per-record inclusion/label reference with evidence pointers and acceptable alternatives.

**Controls:** Clear versus mixed classifications; complete versus selective testing; one valid versus an ambiguous scan/sample linkage. Require a cohort flow table, endpoint policy, excluded/unknown rows, and code. Primary metrics are label and join correctness, not classification accuracy. Authentic case activation depends on inventory proving the required metadata exist.

### C. Image measurement invariance under equivalent representations

**Question:** Can an agent repair a scientifically wrong feature extraction despite a plausible result? Combine F07–F09 with synthetic image phantoms and then verified public images. Transform orientation, spacing, or declared units while preserving known physical anatomy.

**Controls:** Already correct pipeline; known unit or affine error; unrecoverable missing-reference case. Compare volumes and image values in physical space, generated QC overlays, and handling of ambiguity. Phantom invariants give objective truth without asking a model to infer an unpredictable mutation from raw MRI. Real-image clinical accuracy remains a separate question.

### D. Evaluate a claim across null, confounded, and real-signal cases

**Question:** Does an agent distinguish a defensible positive finding from a plausible artifact, without always rejecting? Use matched data-generating processes for F13/F14/F17/F19/F25. Precompute exact or high-precision reference analyses of each finite sample, with legitimate uncertainty.

**Controls:** Null, confounding-only, conditional signal, and insufficient-information instances; same superficial file layout and similar marginal effect sizes where feasible. Score validity of the method and calibrated claim, not whether significance matches the latent generating truth in every sample. This is a harder extension of the earlier audit only if the agent must choose and implement its analysis without being handed the procedure; otherwise retain it as a control.

### E. Freeze an analysis plan, then evaluate a new fixture

**Question:** Does an agent honor its own scientific protocol when attractive results tempt it to change endpoints, exclusions, or thresholds? Have the agent write a machine-readable plan, lock its hash, and only then reveal the evaluation input through a controller. Include F12/F16/F17/F19/F20/F23.

**Controls:** Valid planned positive result, valid planned negative result, and an evaluation failure requiring an explicitly labeled protocol amendment. Score adherence, transparent exploratory departures, and reproducible reports. A computational holdout from one public cohort is not an external clinical validation cohort. Any online adaptive evaluation must retain its full history and not recycle exposed holdouts as pristine tests.

## Grading and isolation contract

**Required submission artifacts:** executable entry point; environment manifest; cohort/endpoint ledger; split membership and fit provenance; frozen per-patient predictions where applicable; machine-readable metrics with definitions; analysis/search ledger; claim-to-artifact/source evidence table; short limitations report. Keep pseudonymous clinical identifiers out of public result bundles unless permitted; aggregate benchmark results usually suffice.

**Objective grading:** file and schema contracts, identifiers/cardinality, exact labels under an explicit policy, unit/geometry invariants, forbidden split overlap, numerical metrics recomputed from predictions, clean execution, unchanged-input determinism within declared tolerance, and behavior on hidden equivalent/perturbed fixtures. Inspect actual code behavior rather than searching for method names. A correct number with non-running or fabricated supporting code does not satisfy an execution task.

**Expert grading:** eligibility ambiguity, estimand choice, whether a confounder adjustment is appropriate, calibration of conclusions, evidence relevance, and clinical applicability. Author accepted equivalence classes before model runs. Two blinded reviewers independently score a stratified sample including all alleged severe failures; adjudicate disagreements and report agreement with uncertainty. Label a disagreement “unresolved,” not a model failure, until settled. A calibrated LLM judge may triage prose; it cannot be the sole judge of the headline failure rate.

**Report a vector, not only an average:** (1) computational correctness, (2) scientific validity, (3) artifact reproducibility, (4) claim support, (5) unnecessary refusal, and (6) critical-error incidence. A high prose score cannot compensate for fabricated evidence or patient leakage. Publish any composite weights and critical-error gates before final testing. Do not equate ordinary error with intentional misconduct.

**Artifact isolation:** evaluate each attempt in a fresh workspace containing only allowed inputs and tools. Keep generators, reference outputs, hidden fixtures, graders, previous solutions, developer logs, host credentials, and other patients/tasks outside the mount. Prevent write access to the grader. Use a separate trusted process to rerun submissions from scratch after deleting generated outputs. Limit access to the approved source corpus when testing reasoning rather than web retrieval; log any permitted network retrieval. [METR's reward-hacking observations](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/) motivate trace inspection and grader isolation; a good numerical score alone is insufficient evidence of valid behavior.

Public source data and old papers are potentially in training data. New task composition and hidden transforms reduce direct answer copying but do not prove contamination-free evaluation. Report source publication dates, release hashes, task authorship dates, permitted retrieval, and when prompts/reference solutions became public. Rotate task families, not merely seed values, for subsequent evaluations.

## Fair tool, time, and experiment budgets

- Supply a tested Python environment and required numerical/imaging packages, local data dictionaries, sufficient disk, and reference runtime measurements. Network/package installation failures should not dominate the score.
- Start tabular audit tasks with a provisional **30-minute wall-clock budget**, and imaging/repository tasks with **60 minutes**, then calibrate before freezing. Require a competent expert to finish within the allowance and use reference compute time as a lower bound; reserve time for at least two complete edit-run-inspect cycles. These are proposed budgets, not measured requirements. If an honest solution needs more time, increase the budget or narrow the task.
- Use CPU/precomputed-feature tasks for the first suite. Full foundation-model training on 16 GB of images is a different resource profile. If adding GPU tasks, publish accelerator, memory, training allowance, and matched access for every system.
- Record exact model identifier, date, reasoning setting, scaffold, tool permissions, context management, token and wall-clock consumption, retries, and estimated cost. Evaluate two or more currently available frontier agent configurations before a cross-model claim; verify availability at run time rather than assuming literature-era models remain frontier.
- Predeclare retries and distinguish environment outage, invalid task/grader, budget exhaustion, and substantive error. Keep the original attempt record. A budget-exhaustion result is a capability-at-that-budget result; it does not alone prove a scientific reasoning failure.

## Bounded autoresearch and uncertainty

1. **Author and validate:** select three initial families from A–C; write one reference solution, a clean counterpart, a deliberately naive control, and independent verification for each. Reject fixtures whose result depends on undocumented assumptions. Confirm that the clean control is genuinely solvable and that the fault control fails for the intended reason.
2. **Development loop:** run a small fixed set of instances; inspect traces; change one task/harness hypothesis at a time; record every attempted change and failure. Separate fixes to a broken environment/grader from scientific task improvements. Keep the earlier 12/12 result in the public experiment history.
3. **Freeze:** hash task source, prompts, allowed tools, budgets, reference standard, grader and exclusion rules. Use new patients/seeds as within-family tests, and separately hold out complete failure mechanisms or task compositions. Do not label post-hoc prompt changes as zero-shot held-out evaluation.
4. **Pilot final runs:** use at least three stochastic attempts per selected instance when budget permits. A useful small pilot is three families × two control conditions × two independent instances × three repetitions × two agents = 72 attempts. This is a workload estimate, not permission to spend or a promise of power. A smaller 12–24-attempt run can establish illustrative failures but supports narrower prevalence claims.
5. **Adjudicate:** confirm each alleged severe failure by replay and independent expert review. Run a documented self-check/prompt-repair arm only after the primary arm is fixed; report it as an intervention, not a replacement for unfavorable runs. A failure erased by a reasonable clarification is different from a persistent methodological failure.
6. **Report:** show per-family and per-control counts with all attempt outcomes. The sampling unit for claims about task generalization is the independently authored family/instance, not every CV fold, gene, seed, or repeated attempt. With enough families use a hierarchical/cluster bootstrap; with very few families report descriptive uncertainty and refrain from precise population claims. A binomial interval across correlated attempts would be misleading. Repeated CV prediction records do not increase the number of clinical patients.
7. **Stop or pivot:** stop development after a predeclared iteration/time/spend cap. If calibrated frontier agents solve the proposed suite, report that result and retain it as a regression benchmark. Do not manufacture difficulty by reducing tools, hiding necessary definitions, tightening time after seeing success, or demanding impossible biological prediction.

## Launch criteria and claims allowed

### Minimum hackathon package

1. A locally verified Valkyrie-compatible task package with the realistic mission, permitted inputs/environment, executable verifier, and isolated reference solution; successful local validation is distinct from hosted execution.
2. One flawed instance and one clean counterpart, plus at least one unexposed fixture verifying that the submitted repair works beyond the visible data. Keep hidden grader material outside the agent workspace.
3. A complete frontier run with exact agent configuration, adequate time/tools, and a reproducible substantive failure. If the agent passes, report that outcome and move to the next validated candidate rather than tightening the budget to force a miss.
4. A short demo: show the plausible original claim; the agent's concrete erroneous artifact; the hidden check and scientific consequence; the reference repair; and the clean control. Show real recorded outcomes, never hypothetical failure presented as a run.
5. A transparent status slide separating completed local execution, completed Valkyrie execution, and any remaining platform work. Cross-model breadth and the larger 72-attempt research protocol described above are stretch validation, not prerequisites to building the first honest demonstration.

Before calling this a frontier failure benchmark, require: verified public-data provenance and permitted artifact handling; reference solutions passing independent tests; clean/negative controls; a successful sandbox replay; at least one reproducible, adjudicated substantive failure by a current frontier configuration with adequate tools/budget; and a held-out evaluation whose exact scope is stated. The broader claim “models systematically fail this class of clinical research” needs multiple independently authored tasks and representative sampling, not one striking example.

For the hackathon, the defensible initial claim would be: “We evaluate whether research agents produce and repair scientifically valid PCNSL analyses, with executable evidence and matched controls.” Replace that with a concrete observed failure statement only after experiments support it. Public-data difficulty, clinical importance, and a polished slide deck are motivations; none alone demonstrates a capability gap.
